How it works

The agent interviews

Claude Code, Cursor, Codex or Copilot words each question, renders it as a picker or a numbered list, takes free-text answers and asks about risks in its own voice.

The server referees

The ontology, the comparison library, the scoring and the choice of the next question live in a deterministic MCP server. It never calls a model and needs no API key.

Modules drop away

Answer "not in my game" to the economy and its axes are gone. Gate questions come first, so you never answer for systems your game does not have.

Try it

The same engine and the same {{comparisons}} verified comparisons as the plugin, running in your browser. Nothing is sent anywhere. In Claude Code the agent words the questions and catches nuance; here you get them as the server sends them.

Under the hood

Belief per axis
Each two-pole axis holds a probability over five bins, from strong A to strong B. Picking A multiplies in 0.50, 0.30, 0.14, 0.04, 0.02; a blend multiplies in 0.05, 0.20, 0.50, 0.20, 0.05.
Archetypes
A posterior over {{archetypes}} archetypes (soulslike action RPG, colony sim, roguelike deckbuilder and more) updates from the same answers and fills in every axis you have not been asked.
Gating
Gate axes set flags such as has_combat or is_multiplayer. A module whose flag is false is skipped whole. Gating is three-valued, so an axis behind an unanswered gate waits.
Next question
Importance times expected information gain on the axis, plus half the information gain on the archetypes, plus gate and familiarity bonuses. A per-session seed picks from the top three, so a session replays exactly.
When to stop
Clarity per axis is 1 minus normalized entropy. The server recommends stopping at overall clarity 0.80 with every frame and gate axis resolved, or after 60 questions.
Tensions
{{tensions}} rules fire as answers arrive, such as physics melee in online PvP or an MMO on a solo team, and come back with a probe question for the agent to ask.

What you get at the end

A GDD seed brief in Markdown or JSON, written by the server from your answers alone. It states only what you decided or what the evidence strongly implies, and lists the rest as open questions.

  1. Frame
  2. Experience targets
  3. Genre
  4. Design pillars
  5. Core loop
  6. Systems spec
  7. Comparable titles
  8. USPs and non-goals
  9. Scope
  10. Risks
  11. Open questions
  12. Decision log

Read a full sample brief from a 45-question session.

5. Core loop

  • Seconds: the player fights under constant pressure, reading the situation and executing in the moment; combat is physics-simulated.
  • Minutes: a session of under 20 minutes is one or more runs that reset on failure, toward goals the player sets.
  • Weeks: mastery and variety keep the player returning.

10. Risks

  • warn: Scope: 18 active modules for a team of 2 to 5 on a timeline of 1 to 2 years. Cut to the pillars (Camera view, Combat range, Failure penalty) for the MVP.

The library

Every comparison is one axis, two or three games, and a short description of how each game handles that axis and nothing else. Each option cites a store page, official site, wiki or review.

{{written}} comparisons are written and {{comparisons}} are verified against their sources. Only verified ones are served. An axis without one falls back to a plain question about its two poles.

Some axes still have only two or three verified comparisons. Sourcing them is the most useful contribution: read the library brief and run npm run coverage.
